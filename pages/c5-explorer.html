Three benches for Course 5: launch planets into orbit, explore real stars on the HR diagram, and measure the expansion of the universe.

What you're practising

Launch speed is shown as a percentage of the circular-orbit speed √(GM/r). At 100% the orbit is a circle; slower gives an ellipse that dips inwards; faster stretches outwards; at √2 ≈ 141% the planet escapes. Equal areas in equal times: watch it speed up near the star. Reader §5.2.

Challenge

Predict, then test: (1) what launch speed escapes? (2) Double the star mass at 1 AU: by what factor does the circular period change?

What you're practising

Real stars on the HR diagram: luminosity against surface temperature, hot on the left. Click any star for its data. The slider places a main-sequence star by mass, using L ∝ M^3.5 and lifetime ≈ 10¹⁰ × M^−2.5 years. Reader §5.5.

Challenge

Click the star you can find in the Southern Cross pointers: Alpha Centauri A. How does it compare with the Sun? Then find a red supergiant and a white dwarf.

What you're practising

Fit the line by eye: adjust the gradient until the residuals (red ticks) are as small and balanced as possible. The gradient is the Hubble constant H₀, and 1/H₀ estimates the age of the universe. Reader §5.8.

Challenge

Data are simulated around the modern value with realistic scatter from galaxies’ own motions.
