Rearranging Formulas Workbook

Thirty-eight problems in seven sets, from one-step rearrangements to linearising real data. Answers are checked by substituting random numbers, so any algebraically correct form is accepted.

How to type answers. Use * for multiply (or leave a space), / for divide, ^ for powers, sqrt( ), cbrt( ), ln( ), log10( ) and pi. Subscripts become plain names: N-nought is N0, R-one is R1. A preview under the box shows how your expression was read; if it looks wrong, add brackets.
  • Try each problem before opening the hint. Use the worked solution to compare method, not just answer.
  • Suggested pacing at four hours a week: Sets A–C in session one, D–E in session two, F–G and a review of anything you missed in session three.
