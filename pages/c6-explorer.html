Three benches for Course 6: genetic crosses, natural selection against drift, and an epidemic model with vaccination.

What you're practising

Each parent passes on one allele at random, so a Punnett square gives probabilities, not guarantees. Breed 100 offspring and compare the counts with the expected ratio: chance makes them wobble. For X-linked genes, sons get their only X from their mother. Reader §6.5.

Challenge

What you're practising

Natural selection pushes a helpful allele up steadily; genetic drift adds random wobble, and in small populations drift can beat selection, even losing a good allele. Set the advantage to 0 to see pure drift. Reader §6.6.

Challenge

Find a population size small enough that a 5% advantage is often lost by chance. Then set a 10% disadvantage: can drift ever carry a harmful allele to fixation in a tiny population?

What you're practising

The SIR model divides a population into Susceptible, Infected and Recovered. An outbreak grows only while each case infects more than one other (R_eff above 1). Vaccination removes susceptible people; past the herd-immunity threshold 1 − 1/R₀ the outbreak fizzles. Reader §6.8.

Challenge

For R₀ = 3, predict the vaccination percentage needed for herd immunity, then test it.
